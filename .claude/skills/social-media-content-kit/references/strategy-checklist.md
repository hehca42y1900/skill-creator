# Content / Promotion Strategy Checklist

Use this before planning a content calendar or a promotion push — writing individual posts without deciding these first tends to produce content that's polished but doesn't add up to anything.

## 1. Goal

What does a "successful" month of content actually move? Pick one primary goal, not three:
- Awareness (new audience reach)
- Engagement (comments, saves, shares from an existing audience)
- Conversion (clicks, sign-ups, sales)
- Retention (keeping an existing customer/community engaged)

The goal decides everything downstream — an awareness push leans on reach-friendly formats (short video, trends), a conversion push leans on offers and clear CTAs.

## 2. Audience

- Who specifically is this for? ("women 25-40 interested in skincare" beats "everyone")
- What do they already believe, and what's the one thing this content should shift?
- Where are they actually active — this decides platform priority, not the brand's preference

## 3. Content pillars

3-5 recurring themes the channel keeps coming back to, so followers know what to expect and it's not a random grab-bag. For example, for a small skincare brand: (1) product education, (2) behind-the-scenes/making-of, (3) customer stories/UGC, (4) offers/announcements. Ask the user for their pillars rather than inventing generic ones — the answer is usually already implicit in what they've been posting or want to be known for.

## 4. Cadence and format mix

- How often can this realistically be sustained? A cadence that looks good on a plan but burns out after two weeks is worse than a slower one that's kept up
- Which pillars get which formats — not every pillar needs every platform
- Leave room for reactive/timely posts (news, trends, replies to comments) alongside the planned pillars

## 5. Voice and consistency

Point to `tone-of-voice.md` — a content calendar full of good ideas still reads as scattered if every post sounds like a different person wrote it.

## 6. Measurement

- What will actually get checked after a month — saves? Link clicks? DMs? Sales attributed to a promo code?
- Pick metrics that map back to the goal in step 1, not just whatever the platform's dashboard shows first

## Output

When the user wants this turned into an actual calendar/plan, structure it as a table: date/week, platform, pillar, format, one-line topic, CTA. Don't write full post copy for every calendar slot unless asked — the calendar is a planning tool, not a batch of finished posts.
