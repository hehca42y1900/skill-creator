# Platform Templates

Each platform rewards a different shape. Use these as a starting logic, not a fill-in-the-blank form — vary the wording so posts don't all sound machine-generated.

## Telegram

Telegram readers are already subscribed — there's no algorithm deciding whether they see the post, so the job of the opening line is to earn a *read*, not a click-through. Supports HTML/Markdown formatting (bold, italics, links, code blocks) and longer posts than the other two.

**Structure:**
```
[Hook — 1 line, specific and concrete, not generic excitement]

[Context/body — 2-5 short paragraphs or a bulleted list.
Telegram readers scan; break up dense text with line breaks.]

[Payoff — what this means for the reader, concretely]

[CTA — one clear action: reply, click, forward, comment]
```

**Example:**
```
Скидка 30% на всё до воскресенья — но не поэтому пишу.

Мы наконец завезли размер XL, на который последний месяц
жаловались в комментариях. Весь ассортимент, все цвета.

Кто ждал — сейчас самое время.

👉 Ссылка в описании канала
```

**Norms:** emoji used sparingly as visual anchors, not decoration on every line. Links can go inline or at the end — inline if the post references something specific, at the end if it's a general CTA.

## VK

VK skews slightly older and more mixed than Instagram, and community/group posts matter as much as personal reach — a post that gets reposted into other communities travels further than one relying on VK's own feed algorithm. Native video/photo posts outperform external links.

**Structure:**
```
[Opening line — can be more conversational/direct than Instagram,
VK audience tolerates plain statements better than forced hooks]

[Body — can be longer than Instagram, similar to Telegram in length,
but write for a feed context: front-load the point]

[Social proof or community angle, if relevant —
VK audiences respond to "look what our community did" more than
polished brand voice]

[CTA — repost/comment/join community, not just "buy now"]
```

**Norms:** native VK polls and albums perform well as a supplement — mention them if the user is planning around a post, not just writing one. Hashtags exist but matter far less than on Instagram — don't over-index on them.

## Instagram

The caption is fighting the feed algorithm and a hard visual truncation — most apps cut the caption after roughly 125 characters before "more". Everything before that cutoff has to work as a complete, standalone hook.

**Structure:**
```
[Hook — must land in ~125 characters, before truncation.
Question, bold claim, or specific number works better than a
general statement.]
⁠
[Body — short lines, lots of white space/line breaks,
1-3 short paragraphs max]

[CTA — comment/save/share, specific ask beats "learn more"]

.
.
.
[Hashtags — 3-8 relevant ones beats 30 generic ones;
place them after a few blank lines or in the first comment
if the user's channel does that]
```

**Example (first ~125 chars matter most):**
```
Купила этот крем 3 раза подряд, потому что забывала,
что уже есть один дома 👇

[body about why it's worth remembering this time]

Сохрани пост, чтобы не забыть в следующий раз 📌

.
.
.
#уходзакожей #косметика #бьютиблог
```

**Norms:** don't put the CTA and hashtags in the same visual block — separate with blank lines (Instagram doesn't render markdown, but blank lines still create visual breathing room). If the account posts hashtags in the first comment instead of the caption, ask the user which convention this channel uses before assuming.

## Cross-platform posts

If the user wants the same announcement adapted to all three, write Telegram first (it has the most room), then compress for VK, then rewrite Instagram's opening from scratch rather than truncating the Telegram hook — a good Telegram opener rarely survives being cut to 125 characters.
