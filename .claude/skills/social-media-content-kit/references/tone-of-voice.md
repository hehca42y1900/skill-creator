# Tone-of-Voice Framework

A tone-of-voice doc is only useful if it can actually tell you whether a specific sentence fits or doesn't — vague adjectives like "friendly and professional" don't do that, because almost every brand would claim them. The framework below forces specificity.

## 1. Three traits, each with a contrast

Pick 3 traits that describe this brand's voice, and for each one, say what it is *not* — the contrast is what makes it checkable.

Example:
- **Direct** — not blunt or cold. Gets to the point fast, but still warm.
- **Playful** — not silly or unserious. Light touch, never at the expense of clarity.
- **Confident** — not arrogant. States things plainly instead of hedging, but doesn't oversell.

If the user doesn't have traits defined yet, ask for 2-3 competitor or reference brands whose voice they like or dislike — it's often faster to react to examples than to generate traits from scratch.

## 2. Say it / don't say it

For each trait, give a concrete before/after so the difference is checkable against real copy, not just describable in the abstract.

Example (for "Direct — not blunt"):
- ❌ Too blunt: "Buy it. It's good."
- ❌ Too soft/hedgy: "We think you might possibly enjoy trying this out sometime!"
- ✅ On-voice: "This is our best-selling cream for a reason — try it."

## 3. Mechanics

Small concrete rules save more inconsistency than trait descriptions do:
- Formal or informal address (ты/вы, first name or not)
- Emoji: none, sparing, or heavy — and where (end of sentence vs. inline)
- Sentence length bias — short and punchy, or longer and explanatory
- Humor: allowed or not, and how far (self-deprecating vs. observational vs. none)

## 4. Checking a draft against the voice

When reviewing a post someone else wrote (or checking your own draft before sending it back to the user), don't just say "this doesn't sound right" — point to which trait it violates and why, using the say-it/don't-say-it examples as the reference point. That's what makes the framework actionable instead of just a mood board.

## 5. When there's no existing brand voice yet

If this is a new brand/channel with nothing defined, don't invent one silently — draft 2-3 trait options based on what the user has said about the product/audience, show short example lines for each, and let them pick or mix. A voice that's picked in 5 minutes by reacting to examples is more likely to stick than one over-engineered in the abstract.
